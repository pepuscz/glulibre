# Evidence and limits

Libre Debug is a learning tool for personal observation, not a promise of disease prevention or longevity.

## Individual responses

The PREDICT study found substantial differences between people's glucose responses to identical meals. That supports examining personal observations, but does not validate this app or establish that one ingredient caused a change. [Berry et al., Nature Medicine](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-020-0934-0).

The app compares whole meals and repeated observations. A typical rise appears only after usable observations across at least three days. Similar meals can still differ in timing, portions, activity and other factors.

## Longevity motivation

David Sinclair discusses food, metabolism and CGMs in *Lifespan*. These discussions motivate curiosity; they are not a validated app protocol. No affiliation or endorsement is claimed. [Original episode](https://www.lifespanpodcast.com/what-to-eat-when-to-eat-for-longevity/), CGM segment at 33:26.

A flatter curve is not a proven longer life. A 2026 systematic review found no appreciable glycemic benefit in healthy normoglycemic participants, despite some encouraging findings in other non-diabetic groups. Not everyone needs a sensor. [Systematic review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41588451/).

## What the app does—and does not—measure

- The sensor supplies glucose measurements; AI identifies foods and estimates nutrition.
- Workouts and sleep duration are optional context from Apple Health. Automated sleep–glucose analysis is not implemented.
- Nutrition estimates need review. One excursion or the chart's reference band is not a diagnosis or an individual treatment target.
- The app is not intended to guide medication or replace prescribed monitoring.
